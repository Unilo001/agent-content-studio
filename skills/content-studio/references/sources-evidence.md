# Sources and evidence strength

Principles are hypotheses anchored in what was read and in the approver's rejections. Own data (small n) shows no certain causes. Mark every claim you rely on with a grade.

## Grades
- **A** primary source read in full, similar context (e.g. platform's own guidance).
- **B** primary source read, different context (classroom studies, news sites, TV subtitles).
- **C** summary or secondary source.
- **D** creator blog or book, not opened.
- **K** your own numbers (state n).
- **U** direct feedback from the approver or audience.

## What the principles lean on
- Coherence effect (removing interesting-but-irrelevant material helps learning): Mayer & Moreno, multimedia learning [B]. Segmenting effect applies to learner-paced segments, not to short video.
- Curiosity needs a focused gap and prior knowledge: Loewenstein 1994 [B].
- Prediction before demonstration: Crouch et al. 2004 [B]; moderate effect only.
- Reading speed for non-fiction ~238 words/min: Brysbaert 2019 [A]. Subtitles aid comprehension [B/C]. Same words in speech and text reduce transfer (redundancy) [B].
- Slow motion lowers perceived activation: Wollner et al. 2018 [B].
- Platforms say they rank on watch time, likes, shares/sends and de-prioritise unoriginal reposted content; first seconds drive skip rate [A, platform guidance].

## Do not cite as fact
Best posting time; "sends are worth N times likes"; "a skip rate under X % is healthy"; "N % of viewers who pass 3 s reach 10 s"; "N % watch without sound"; "layered hooks multiply hold time"; the Zeigarnik effect as a reason for open loops; that repeated templates *provably* cost followers; any weights the platform has not published; the effect of unposted drafts. If you need a working target, label it "working target, no platform evidence" and test it with your own data.

## How to add a lesson
1. Write the observation with date, the piece, n and grade (K or U).
2. Hypothesis in one line.
3. If it should change behaviour, propose it to the orchestrator, who edits the owning skill or BRAND.md "Taste rules learned". Workers do not edit shared skills.
